# Kuşköy short (v1, ~40 s)  — Black Sea hills, Turkey. Style: Tiny Globe. Characters: PIN (host), FARMER (Hasan, whistler), SKEPTIC (city man)

## Ch1 – hook (valley, morning mist, hazelnut trees)
PIN: This Turkish village has three hundred ninety-three people, and they talk across a whole valley without a phone.
SKEPTIC: Impossible. Whistling is not a language.
FARMER: [whistles] It is Turkish. Every syllable becomes a note.

## Ch2 – how
PIN: Those notes carry more than half a mile.
SKEPTIC: Why not just shout?
FARMER: A shout dies in the hills. A whistle does not.
PIN: Four hundred years ago, farmers on these steep slopes had no other way to call each other.

## Ch3 – doubt and turn
SKEPTIC: But today everyone has a phone.
FARMER: Yes. And now fewer people whistle.
PIN: You see, scientists found that whistlers use both sides of their brain to talk, not just one.

## Ch4 – button
PIN: So the village now teaches whistling in school, and holds a whistling festival every year since nineteen ninety-seven.
FARMER: [whistles]
PIN: What did he say?
FARMER: Dinner.

## Facts to keep (sources)
393 inhabitants (2022), ~400-year-old whistled language (Wikipedia/TÜIK); audible >0.5 mile (NOVA/PBS); cell phones reduced use (Wikipedia, Daily Sabah); whistlers use both brain hemispheres (NOVA/PBS); festival since 1997; taught in schools. "Dinner" = joke, say so in the description.
