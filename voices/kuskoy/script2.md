# Kuşköy short v2 (~45 s). Hook = question. Locals speak English with a Turkish accent (tr-TR voices).
# Cast: PIN (host), HASAN (old hazelnut farmer, flat cap, moustache), AYSE (woman, headscarf, tea), TOURIST (city visitor)

## 1 hook (Turkish flag + title card, then valley)
PIN: Did you know there's a village in Turkey where people talk by whistling?
WHISTLE: 1.2
PIN: That whistle just said: Hasan, come for tea!
WHISTLE: 0.9
HASAN: I am coming!

## 2 what (village lane, wooden houses)
PIN: The village is called Kuşköy. It means bird village.
PIN: Every Turkish word can be turned into a whistle.
TOURIST: Any word?
AYSE: Any word. Even your name.
WHISTLE: 1.0

## 3 why (steep hazelnut slopes)
PIN: Why? The mountains here are so steep, walking to your neighbour can take an hour.
HASAN: A shout gets lost in the valley. A whistle carries more than half a mile.
PIN: People here have whistled like this for about four hundred years.

## 4 turn (tea house)
TOURIST: But today everyone has a phone.
AYSE: Yes. That is why young people stopped whistling.
PIN: So in twenty seventeen, UNESCO put it on its list of heritage in urgent danger.
PIN: Now kids learn it at school, and the village holds a whistling festival every year.

## 5 button (festival)
TOURIST: So what did she just whistle about me?
HASAN: She says you talk too much.

---
## Facts (sources)
Kuşköy (Çanakçı, Giresun) ~393 inhabitants; name = "bird village"; whistled Turkish ~400 years; carries >0.5 mile (NOVA/PBS); UNESCO Urgent Safeguarding List 2017 (ich.unesco.org/en/USL/00658); mobile phones main threat (UNESCO); courses since 2014, schools; annual festival since 1997. Whistle meanings in hook/button are illustrative jokes – say so in description.
